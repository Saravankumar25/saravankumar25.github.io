The story before the details

Imagine a machine with no operating system, screen, keyboard, or even a concept of the number seven. All it has is a way to distinguish physical states. That is enough of a starting point. A circuit can receive a high or low signal, combine signals according to fixed relationships, and preserve a state when the right storage structure is present. Meaning is something we assign to those states.

Real electronics are analog underneath our digital descriptions. A voltage might take many values; a logical receiver treats valid ranges as low or high. That is a deliberate abstraction. You should not think of each bit as a tiny bucket containing a literal printed zero or one. It is a persistent or transient physical condition that other circuitry can distinguish.

The fascinating step is composition. A single switch does little. A set of switches can represent a code. A gate can transform that code. A feedback circuit can remember it. A collection of circuits can carry out operations selected by other codes. The end goal is not to memorize device names, but to see how one layer gives the next layer a reliable contract.
