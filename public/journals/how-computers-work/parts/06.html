Memory of a single bit

Cross the gap between circuits that react now and circuits that remember later.

The story before the details

Up to this point, most circuits change their outputs when their inputs change. That is useful for calculating a function, but it does not store anything. A program is a sequence of steps, and sequences require memory of earlier steps. Feedback is the key conceptual change: an output can influence future behavior through a loop.

Latches show that simple logic can hold one bit. Flip-flops make it easier to coordinate many pieces of state around clock transitions. A register combines multiple one-bit storage elements into one named multi-bit value. Nothing is “memorized” in the human sense; stable electrical configurations and clocked transitions preserve the pattern.

The clock is a synchronization mechanism rather than an instruction interpreter. It offers common moments for state transfer, but the hardware still needs correct timing, appropriate enable signals and paths that settle soon enough. Once a register output feeds an incrementer and returns to its own input, we have a counter and an important piece of a CPU.
