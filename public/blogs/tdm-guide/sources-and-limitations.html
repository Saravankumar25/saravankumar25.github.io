A learning guide is only as good as its sources. This page is the honest ledger: what was actually fetched and read, what could not be, and what that means for how you should use each chapter. Nothing here is rounded up.

The short version
  • Delphix: the complete Continuous Compliance help site (352 pages, version 2026.5) was downloaded. The pages that matter for the curriculum (connectors, rule sets, profiling, algorithms, jobs, files, terminology) were read in full by the chapter writers; installation, API and plugin-SDK pages were downloaded but not studied. Screenshots are linked from the pages.
  • GenRocket: the complete help centre (845 pages) and the receivers page on app.genrocket.com were downloaded. The pages for domains, attributes, generators, receivers, scenarios and the runtime were read; areas outside the curriculum (for example EDI, Salesforce, multi-user server, partition engine) were not studied. Screenshots are linked from the pages.
  • Informatica: the official docs site refuses automated access, so the source is the TDM 10.5.10 User Guide PDF (455 pages) supplied by the learner. Figures are cited by PDF page number, not linked as images.
  • YouTube: the three videos' transcripts could not be retrieved. Their content is not used anywhere in this guide.

A.2 How the research was done

  1. Diagnose the block.

    Outbound web access was first denied by the cloud session's network policy. After the policy was opened, Delphix, GenRocket and YouTube's public pages worked. The Informatica docs site still returned 403, which is the site's own access control, so no attempt was made to evade it.

  2. Enumerate every page, not just the linked ones.

    Delphix publishes a machine-readable table of contents; GenRocket publishes a site map. Both were used to download every page, rather than only the pages named in the curriculum.

  3. Verify every image URL.

    Each screenshot link was requested and had to return HTTP 200 before it could be used. Embedded screenshots also fall back to a plain link if your browser cannot load them.

  4. Write from the files.

    Chapter writers read the extracted text for their topics and cite the page they used in each chapter's "Sources used" box. Where a source did not say something, the chapters say so instead of guessing.

  5. Machine-check the links.

    The build refuses to publish a link that is not on the verified list, except a small set of labelled leads.

A.3 Limitations you should know about

Read before relying on this guide
  • No video content. Nothing in this guide comes from the three videos. If you want that material added, paste the transcripts (YouTube: "..." then "Show transcript") and a later edition can use them.
  • Informatica visuals are page references. You will see lines like "User Guide p.38, Figure ...". Open your PDF to that page to see the figure.
  • Versions move. Delphix content follows Continuous Compliance 2026.5 and Informatica content follows TDM 10.5.10 (February 2026). Your organisation may run a different version, so a menu label can differ.
  • Lab output is illustrative. Sample data is fake (the fictional ShopSphere company). Labs show what to expect from the documentation; they are not recordings of a real run in your environment.
  • "Cog IPs" and partnership content. The curriculum mentions Cognizant's own tools and partnerships. No source for those was available, so they are not described. Ask your trainer.
  • Regulations. GDPR, HIPAA and similar laws are explained in general terms for learning. This is not legal advice.
  • Generated writing. The chapters were drafted by AI from the sources above and then checked by script. Treat statements about product behaviour as "per the docs", and confirm in your own environment before presenting them as fact.

A.4 How to tell how much to trust a statement

From docs
Taken from an official source named in that chapter's "Sources used" box.
General concept
Standard industry background that does not depend on one product.
Unverified
A lead that could not be fetched or confirmed. Treat with caution.
Illustrative
An example invented for teaching (ShopSphere data, expected outputs). It shows what to look for, not what your system will print.
