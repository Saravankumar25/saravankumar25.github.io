How a real TDM engagement is planned and signed off, the capstone project walk-through, and a glossary of every term used in the guide.

10.1 Planning a real TDM engagement: use cases, POC, assessment and sign-off

Days 43, 48, 54, 57

Knowing how to run a masking job is one skill. Getting a company to trust you with its data, agreeing what "done" means, and leaving behind something the audit team accepts is another, and it is the one that decides whether a TDM project survives. This section is about that second skill. It turns the syllabus items on end-to-end plan development into templates you can fill in: a use-case questionnaire, a data inventory, a sensitivity assessment, a tool-selection matrix, a proof-of-concept (POC) plan, an assessment report, a risk register, a deployment plan, a sign-off checklist, a RACI table, KPIs, and a final-presentation outline.

Everything is worked through with ShopSphere, the fictional online retailer. The numbers and names are invented to show the shape of each document. Where a statement is about what a tool can do, it comes from the sources I read and is marked. Everything else is common project practice and is labelled General concept.

What you will learn
  • How the Day 43, 48, 54 and 57 syllabus items fit one life cycle: discover, assess, choose a tool, prove it, plan, deploy, sign off, present.
  • How to ask the right questions to find TDM use cases, and how to record the answers.
  • How to inventory a data landscape and rate the sensitivity of each column.
  • How Informatica TDM, GenRocket and Delphix differ on what each is best at, using the three sources.
  • How to write a POC plan with success criteria that can be measured.
  • What goes into an assessment report, a risk register, a deployment plan and a sign-off checklist.
  • Who does what (RACI), what to measure (KPIs), how projects fail, and how to present the result.
What this section is based on

From docs statements about the tools come from the Informatica TDM 10.5.10 User Guide (PDF, February 2026, 455 pages), cited as (User Guide 10.5.10, p.NN); the Delphix Continuous Compliance 2026.5 documentation (last published October 16, 2026); and the GenRocket help centre pages named in the sources list. The templates, scoring examples, KPIs, RACI and ShopSphere figures are General concept or my own illustration, not from any vendor. Real organisations use their own formats. Ask your trainer for the one your project must follow.

Day 43 also lists COTS overview, partnership and "Cog IPs". The sources I had do not describe any partnership or any Cognizant proprietary tool, so this section does not either: those are organisation-specific and your trainer is the person to ask.

10.1.1 Words to know first

Engagement
One piece of TDM work for one client or business unit, from first conversation to hand-over. ShopSphere's "protect QA data" project is an engagement.
Use case
A specific situation where test data causes a problem, written in terms of who needs what data and why. "QA needs 90 days of orders without real customer identities" is a use case.
TDG
Test data generation: creating synthetic data instead of copying real data. The syllabus abbreviates it that way on Day 48.
LOB
Line of business: one business area with its own applications and data, such as ShopSphere's order management.
Technology landscape
The list of systems, databases, files and interfaces that hold or move the data in scope. The "map" you draw before you choose tools.
POC (proof of concept)
A small, time-boxed trial of the chosen approach on a slice of real systems, to show it works before a full rollout.
Assessment
A structured review of an application and its data environment, ending in a written report with findings and recommendations.
Sign-off
Formal written approval by named people that agreed criteria have been met.
Success criteria
Measurable statements that decide pass or fail, agreed before the POC starts.
Risk register
A living table of things that could go wrong, with likelihood, impact, owner and mitigation.
RACI
A table that marks, for each task, who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted or Informed.
KPI
Key performance indicator: a number you track to show whether TDM is helping.
BU SME
Business-unit subject-matter expert. The syllabus says the BU SME evaluates the final project deliverables.

Why plan before touching a tool

The costly mistakes in TDM are decided early: masking a column that is never used, missing a file that carries the same SSN, picking a tool that cannot read the main database, or promising "all sensitive data masked" without agreeing how to measure it. Each of those is cheap to fix on paper and very expensive after the first refresh has reached 200 testers. General concept

Two plans: generation (Day 48) and masking (Day 54)

Masking plan

Starts from existing data. The central question: which real data can we safely make available, and how do we prove it is protected?

Heavy on: landscape, sensitivity rating, algorithm choice, referential integrity, evidence.

Generation (TDG) plan

Starts from a model of the data. The central question: what shape of data do the tests need, and how much?

Heavy on: data model, rules and patterns, volumes, edge cases, repeatability.

The tools themselves draw the same line. In Informatica TDM a data generation operation needs a separate plan: the guide says generation cannot be combined with masking or subsetting in the same plan (User Guide 10.5.10, p.33). GenRocket describes its own method as Model, Design, Deploy, Manage (The GenRocket Methodology; the page itself carries only a video). Many engagements need both: mask the customer data, generate the data that never existed.

10.1.4 Finding the use cases

A tool can only solve a problem someone has named. The questionnaire below is how you collect the problems. Ask it in short interviews with QA, development, DBA, security and compliance people, not by email. Write the answers down in the same table every time. General concept

10.1.5 Mapping the technology landscape

Day 54 asks you to analyse the technology landscape. In practice that is a table: every place where in-scope data lives or moves, who owns it, and which environments it feeds. Draw it before choosing tools, because a tool that cannot reach one of the rows leaves a hole in the protection. General concept

Then check each row against what the tools can read. The sources say: Informatica TDM builds subsets from relational, flat file or XSD sources (User Guide 10.5.10, p.112). Delphix lists Fixed Width, Delimited, XML, JSON and Parquet files as supported (Files connector) and databases through its connectors. GenRocket's G-Subset subsets and masks MySQL, MSSQL, PostgreSQL, Oracle, DB2, Sybase and Databricks, but only between databases of the same type (G-Subset overview). A row that no candidate tool reads goes straight into the risk register.

The same SSN appears in L1, L3 and L4. That is the central integrity requirement: whatever masks it must give the same result everywhere, or the partner's test data no longer joins to ShopSphere's. The masking concepts are in the masking concepts chapter.

10.1.6 Sensitivity assessment

For each column in scope, decide whether it is sensitive, how badly, and what to do. Do not trust column names alone: both Informatica and Delphix profile the data itself as well as the names. Informatica discovers data domains by patterns in data or metadata (User Guide 10.5.10, p.31). Delphix's ASDD profiler adds data-level classifiers, which found fields called DATA_00 and DATA_01 as phone and email in an official screenshot (see the Delphix profiling chapter). A simple, defensible rating is impact if leaked (1 to 3) times exposure in test (1 to 3). General concept

The decision rule (my illustration): score 6 or more gets masked; below that is a business discussion. The compliance officer, not the TDM engineer, owns the final "keep" decisions. Record each decision with a reason, because it will be questioned in the sign-off.

10.1.8 The proof of concept

A POC answers one question: will this approach work here, on our data, with our people? It is small on purpose. It uses a copy, never production, and it ends with a pass or fail against criteria written before it started. A POC without agreed criteria turns into a demo that everyone declares a success. General concept

POC plan for ShopSphere

SectionContent for ShopSphere (illustrative)
ObjectiveShow that the chosen tool can mask the eight sensitive CUSTOMERS columns and one export file on a lab copy, keep keys and formats intact, and run without manual fixes

EnvironmentIsolated lab, masking user with only the rights the tool documents, no route to production

Time boxTen working days: 2 setup, 3 profile and rule design, 2 masking runs and fixes, 2 verification, 1 report
TeamTDM engineer (runs it), DBA (copy and rights), data steward (rules), compliance officer (accepts evidence)
ExitPass if every "must" criterion is met; otherwise a written list of gaps and a decision: fix, change tool, or stop

Delphix leaves non-conformant values unmasked by default and records a warning, so a job can finish while data stays exposed (see Delphix masking terminology). Informatica's SSN masking is documented to be unable to return all-unique values (User Guide 10.5.10, p.175), which is exactly the sort of limit a POC should expose before rollout.

10.1.9 The assessment of a line of business

Day 54 ends with an assessment on "a particular LOB". The POC proves the tool; the assessment describes the business area it will be applied to, and what must change for a full rollout. For ShopSphere the LOB is online order management. Work through the dimensions below and record evidence for each. General concept

DimensionWhat to find out
Application and interfacesWhich applications use the data, which files and APIs leave the LOB, which hold copies
DataTables, volumes, growth, real keys and undeclared relationships (a join the application uses but the database does not declare breaks masking)
Environments and refreshHow many, how often, who triggers it, current duration
Existing controlsCurrent masking scripts, access rules, past incidents
ComplianceApplicable rules and what evidence an auditor will ask for
Fit with toolsLandscape rows against tool support, from the matrix
InfrastructureCapacity and network. Delphix's best-practice page, for example, gives a standard dedicated engine as 8 vCPUs and at least 64 GB of RAM, about one vCPU per concurrent masking job and at least 1 GB per job (Best practices)
People and processWho will own rules, keys and reruns after the project

Assessment report outline

  1. Summary: scope, verdict (ready, ready with conditions, not ready), the three findings that matter most.
  2. Scope and method: LOB, systems, interviews held, evidence reviewed, POC results.
  3. Current state: the landscape inventory and the use-case register.
  4. Sensitivity results: the column ratings and decisions.
  5. Findings: each with evidence, severity and a recommendation, for example "F3, High: the partner file L3 carries unmasked SSNs; mask before the next nightly run".
  6. Tool recommendation: the filled scoring table and why.
  7. Deployment plan: phases, effort and dependencies (next section).
  8. Risks: the register.
  9. Appendices: evidence pack, queries, configuration exports.

10.1.11 The deployment plan

A deployment plan turns a successful POC into a repeatable service. It names phases, the condition for entering and leaving each one, and who owns it. General concept

PhaseMain activitiesExit condition
P0 MobiliseApprove scope and success criteria; accounts and approvals; baseline KPIs measuredSigned charter and baseline
P1 PrepareSize and install the tool; network routes; masking database users with only needed rights; backup and restore testedSmoke test: connect, profile one table
P2 ConfigureConnectors, rule sets, profiling, inventory review, algorithms, keys or seeds, roles; for Informatica, policies, projects and plansInventory approved by the data steward
P3 Pilot the LOBRun the full LOB in one environment; verify with the POC criteriaEvidence pack accepted
P4 Roll outRemaining environments and files; schedule jobs; connect to the refresh process; train usersSign-off checklist complete
P5 OperateMonitor jobs, re-profile on releases, report KPIs, review risksQuarterly review

Some tool features support promotion. Delphix lets you export an environment, with its connectors, rule sets and jobs, and import it into another masking engine; the docs say moving environments between engines is the main reason (Managing environments). It also offers an inventory approval workflow that makes an approver sign off a database inventory before any masking job can run. Informatica generates a workflow from a plan and runs it (User Guide 10.5.10, p.32). Use these to move work from the pilot to production setups without re-typing it.

The refresh runbook

The safest ordering is: copy into a closed area, mask, verify, and only then open the copy to testers. Nobody sees unmasked data at any point. General concept Delphix's masked provisioning enforces a version of this for supported databases by masking during VDB provisioning (see Provision masked VDBs).

The generation (TDG) deployment plan

For Day 48 the same phases apply with different content. GenRocket describes a hybrid design: modelling and design happen in the cloud component, and the data itself is generated locally in the on-premises component (GenRocket architecture). Its five components are domain, attribute, generator, receiver and scenario (five key components).

StepFor ShopSphere
ModelDomains for CUSTOMERS, ORDERS, ORDER_ITEMS, PRODUCTS; attributes per column; parent-child links

DeployRuntime on the generation machine, a receiver that loads the QA database or writes files, access to the target
ManageVersion the scenarios, rerun on schema change, track generation time and test coverage gained

10.2.6 Activity 3: profile and mask SSN, alphanumeric values, TaxID and ZipCode

Brief

Goal. Mask four kinds of identifying value so the data stays useful: formats are kept, the same real value always becomes the same masked value, and keys and joins still work. Inputs. CUSTOMERS with ssn, membership_code, tax_id and zip_code; the customer extract customers.dat (which carries the SSN); the "before" copy; and the edge row 1004 with SSN N/A. Tool. Delphix primary; Informatica as a cross-check. Output. Four verified columns, an algorithm sheet where every line says "docs" or "design", and the evidence.

The word "integrity" has two meanings here, and you must show both. Referential integrity: foreign keys still match. None of these four columns is a key, so the safest decision is not to mask any key at all. Consistency: the same SSN or TaxID appears in several places (the database and the extract file) and must mask to the same value everywhere, or joins between systems fail. Deterministic algorithms, with the same algorithm instance and key used everywhere, give consistency.

Algorithm design, column by column

  • Per activity: inventory export, algorithm or entity configuration, query and shell output, job summaries or reports, and a decision note per column or table.
  • Algorithm sheet: one line per column: algorithm, parameters, reason, and "docs" or "design".
  • Final report (two to four pages): scope, approach, results against each acceptance criterion, warnings and how they were explained, limits, and what you would do next, following the assessment report outline.
  • Presentation for Day 57, following the slide outline in the same chapter.

10.2.8 What evaluators look for

The syllabus describes the evaluation only as the BU SME judging the deliverables; I have no marking scheme, so this section is my general reading of what a reviewer of masking work needs to see, not a published rubric. Four things recur.

  1. Proof, not claims. Each acceptance criterion has a query result or report next to it. A statement such as "SSN is masked" without a before/after comparison counts for little.
  2. Understanding of why. You can say why you chose an algorithm, what it guarantees (deterministic, unique, format-keeping) and what it does not (for example, that the FirstName algorithm can return the input, or that nothing I read says CM Digits yields valid SSNs).
  3. Integrity and safety. Keys and joins survive, the same value masks the same way in the database and the file, nothing real is left behind, and the work was done on a restorable copy.
  4. Honesty about limits. Warnings are explained, assumptions and design choices are labelled, and open items are listed. Separating documented fact from your own choice is a strength, not a weakness.

10.2.11 Common mistakes and gotchas

Where capstones lose marks
  • Masking a key to "be safe". It breaks joins. Leave keys alone unless the same deterministic algorithm is applied on both sides.
  • Different algorithms for the same value. An SSN masked differently in the database and the file ruins consistency; reuse one instance.
  • Numeric columns and leading zeros. Digit masking on a numeric zip or tax id can collide; keep such values as text or use the numeric algorithm.
  • Trusting profiling for six-digit postal codes. The documented ZIP data patterns target US formats; the column name does the work.
  • A subset left unmasked. It is real data; say how it will be masked.
  • Presenting design choices as product facts. "CM Digits is the SSN algorithm" is false; "I chose CM Digits for SSN because..." is right.

10.2.12 Recap

Recap
  • The project has three activities: names, subset, and SSN, alphanumeric values, TaxID and ZipCode. Plan them over daily steps and log progress every day.
  • Use Delphix for the masking activities and Informatica for the subset, with the other tool as a cross-check and GenRocket for fake source rows; follow your trainer if the assignment differs.
  • Every activity has acceptance criteria that a query or report can prove.
  • Format-preserving, deterministic algorithms (CM Digits, CM Alpha-Numeric, a Character Mapping instance with a preserve range, or Informatica key masking with a mask format and seed) keep shape and consistency. Segment Mapping and Informatica SSN masking are documented alternatives with their own limits.
  • Integrity means both intact foreign keys and the same value masked the same way in every system.
  • A subset is real data until it is masked.
  • Label every decision as documented or designed, and explain every warning.
