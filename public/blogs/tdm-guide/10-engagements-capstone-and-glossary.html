Planning a real TDM engagement: use cases, POC, assessment and sign-off

Knowing how to run a masking job is one skill. Getting a company to trust you with its data, agreeing what "done" means, and leaving behind something the audit team accepts is another, and it is the one that decides whether a TDM project survives. This section covers the documents of a TDM engagement: a use-case questionnaire, a data inventory, a sensitivity assessment, a tool-selection matrix, a proof-of-concept (POC) plan, an assessment report, a risk register, a deployment plan, a sign-off checklist, a RACI table and KPIs.

Everything is worked through with ShopSphere, the fictional online retailer (example data).

10.1 Words to know first

Engagement
One piece of TDM work for one client or business unit, from first conversation to hand-over. ShopSphere's "protect QA data" project is an engagement.
Use case
A specific situation where test data causes a problem, written in terms of who needs what data and why. "QA needs 90 days of orders without real customer identities" is a use case.
TDG
Test data generation: creating synthetic data instead of copying real data.
LOB
Line of business: one business area with its own applications and data, such as ShopSphere's order management.
Technology landscape
The list of systems, databases, files and interfaces that hold or move the data in scope. The "map" you draw before you choose tools.
POC (proof of concept)
A small, time-boxed trial of the chosen approach on a slice of real systems, to show it works before a full rollout.
Assessment
A structured review of an application and its data environment, ending in a written report with findings and recommendations.
Sign-off
Formal written approval by named people that agreed criteria have been met.
Success criteria
Measurable statements that decide pass or fail, agreed before the POC starts.
Risk register
A living table of things that could go wrong, with likelihood, impact, owner and mitigation.
RACI
A table that marks, for each task, who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted or Informed.
KPI
Key performance indicator: a number you track to show whether TDM is helping.
Evidence pack
The queries, screenshots and reports that prove each success criterion is met.

Why plan before touching a tool

The costly mistakes in TDM are decided early: masking a column that is never used, missing a file that carries the same SSN, picking a tool that cannot read the main database, or promising "all sensitive data masked" without agreeing how to measure it. Each of those is cheap to fix on paper and very expensive after the first refresh has reached 200 testers.

10.2.1 Two plans: generation and masking

Masking plan

Starts from existing data. The central question: which real data can we safely make available, and how do we prove it is protected?

Heavy on: landscape, sensitivity rating, algorithm choice, referential integrity, evidence.

Generation (TDG) plan

Starts from a model of the data. The central question: what shape of data do the tests need, and how much?

Heavy on: data model, rules and patterns, volumes, edge cases, repeatability.

The tools themselves draw the same line. In Informatica TDM a data generation operation needs a separate plan: the guide says generation cannot be combined with masking or subsetting in the same plan (User Guide 10.5.10, p.33). GenRocket describes its own method as Model, Design, Deploy, Manage (The GenRocket Methodology; the page itself carries only a video). Many engagements need both: mask the customer data, generate the data that never existed.

Then check each row against what the tools can read. The sources say: Informatica TDM builds subsets from relational, flat file or XSD sources (User Guide 10.5.10, p.112). The same SSN appears in L1, L3 and L4. That is the central integrity requirement: whatever masks it must give the same result everywhere, or the partner's test data no longer joins to ShopSphere's. The masking concepts are in the masking concepts chapter.

10.5 Sensitivity assessment

For each column in scope, decide whether it is sensitive, how badly, and what to do. Do not trust column names alone: both Informatica and Delphix profile the data itself as well as the names. Informatica discovers data domains by patterns in data or metadata (User Guide 10.5.10, p.31). Delphix's ASDD profiler adds data-level classifiers, which found fields called DATA_00 and DATA_01 as phone and email in an official screenshot (see the Delphix labs chapter). A simple, defensible rating is impact if leaked (1 to 3) times exposure in test (1 to 3).

10.7 The proof of concept

A POC answers one question: will this approach work here, on our data, with our people? It is small on purpose. It uses a copy, never production, and it ends with a pass or fail against criteria written before it started. A POC without agreed criteria turns into a demo that everyone declares a success.

10.7.3 How a services partner structures the whole path

The POC is the middle of a longer path. In the 2020 GenRocket and Kualitatem webinar, a representative of Kualitatem, a software-testing services company and GenRocket partner, described how his company structures a TDM engagement in three phases. This is one company's method, told in a sales webinar, not a standard.

Partner's phaseWhat the partner describedMatching section
1. AssessmentBuild the business case. Scan the client's environments, tools and skills. Recommend a TDM tool and give an implementation plan for it.The use-case questionnaire (section 10.3), the landscape inventory (section 10.4), the tool matrix (section 10.6) and the assessment report (section 10.8)
2. Pilot, then roadmap and rolloutPick a medium-sized project, neither the biggest nor the smallest, that covers the key scenarios, and produce results the client can see as measurable improvement. Then lay out a roadmap and roll out project by project, based on the data model.The POC plan and its success criteria (section 10.7), then the deployment plan (section 10.10)
3. SupportAfter go-live, a set period of production support in which the client can bring problems and questions back to the team.The Operate phase of the deployment plan, and the conditions and owners recorded at sign-off (section 10.11)

Two points are worth borrowing. First, choose the pilot for coverage, not for ease: a project that touches the key scenarios shows the client the real benefit, while a trivial one proves little. The ShopSphere POC above is deliberately smaller (a lab copy, ten days); the partner's pilot is a real medium-sized project. Both produce measurable results before a wider rollout. Second, plan the support period before go-live, so the client knows who to call after the project team leaves.

The partner also listed what it weighs when recommending a tool: complexity and cost, speed of providing data, adaptability to the client's existing tools (it named Jenkins, Selenium, CI pipelines and programming languages), and the support available. These map onto the scoring table in section 10.6. The partner added that GenRocket meets all four factors. That is a claim from a vendor's partner, so take scores from your own POC instead.

The webinar ended with the vendor's route to a POC: a personal demo, then a no-cost proof of concept delivered by the vendor, the partner or both. Vendors commonly offer a similar route. Accept it if it helps, but still agree your own success criteria and exit decision first.

10.8.1 Assessment report outline

  1. Summary: scope, verdict (ready, ready with conditions, not ready), the three findings that matter most.
  2. Scope and method: LOB, systems, interviews held, evidence reviewed, POC results.
  3. Current state: the landscape inventory and the use-case register.
  4. Sensitivity results: the column ratings and decisions.
  5. Findings: each with evidence, severity and a recommendation, for example "F3, High: the partner file L3 carries unmasked SSNs; mask before the next nightly run".
  6. Tool recommendation: the filled scoring table and why.
  7. Deployment plan: phases, effort and dependencies (next section).
  8. Risks: the register.
  9. Appendices: evidence pack, queries, configuration exports.

Some tool features support promotion. Delphix lets you export an environment, with its connectors, rule sets and jobs, and import it into another masking engine; the docs say moving environments between engines is the main reason (Managing environments). 10.10.2 The generation (TDG) deployment plan

For generation the same phases apply with different content. Its five components are domain, attribute, generator, receiver and scenario (five key components).

10.22 Part 3: profile and mask SSN, alphanumeric values, TaxID and ZipCode

10.22.1 Scope

Goal. Mask four kinds of identifying value so the data stays useful: formats are kept, the same real value always becomes the same masked value, and keys and joins still work. Inputs. CUSTOMERS with ssn, membership_code, tax_id and zip_code; the customer extract customers.dat (which carries the SSN); the "before" copy; and the edge row 1004 with SSN N/A. Tool. Delphix primary; Informatica as a cross-check. Output. Four verified columns and an algorithm sheet.

The word "integrity" has two meanings here, and you must show both. Referential integrity: foreign keys still match. None of these four columns is a key, so the safest decision is not to mask any key at all. Consistency: the same SSN or TaxID appears in several places (the database and the extract file) and must mask to the same value everywhere, or joins between systems fail. Deterministic algorithms, with the same algorithm instance and key used everywhere, give consistency.

10.23 Common mistakes and gotchas

Common mistakes
  • Masking a key to "be safe". It breaks joins. Leave keys alone unless the same deterministic algorithm is applied on both sides.
  • Different algorithms for the same value. An SSN masked differently in the database and the file ruins consistency; reuse one instance.
  • Numeric columns and leading zeros. Digit masking on a numeric zip or tax id can collide; keep such values as text or use the numeric algorithm.
  • Trusting profiling for six-digit postal codes. The documented ZIP data patterns target US formats; the column name does the work.
  • A subset left unmasked. It is real data; mask it in the same plan.
  • Presenting design choices as product facts. CM Digits is a suitable choice for SSN, not the product's SSN algorithm.

A named folder setting, such as resource.output.directory, each user fills with their own path. Section 6.23
Output format (GenRocket)
The shape of the result: delimited, fixed-width, JSON, XML, Excel, SQL statements and many more. Section 6.22
Parent, child and sibling domains (GenRocket)
A child has exactly one parent whose key it uses; a "describing" lookup table is marked a sibling. Section 6.15
PermutationGen (GenRocket)
Generates all possible combinations of the values it is given. Section 7.6
Preview (GenRocket)
Sample generated values shown in the web platform so you can check before downloading. Section 6.13
Primary attribute (GenRocket)
The attribute marked as the key, which children refer to. Section 6.13
Primary domain and root domain (GenRocket)
The primary domain is the one a scenario is built for; the root is the first parent at the top of the chain. Section 6.22
Project and project version (GenRocket)
A project holds the data model for one application or database; a version is one release's copy of it. Section 6.8
Project type (GenRocket)
Standard (default), Micro (domain-limited, such as files) or IPM (in-place masking of databases). Section 6.8
QueryOnceGen (GenRocket)
A query generator that reads real data from a database once, for example the current maximum id. Section 6.18
Quick Scenario (GenRocket)
Builds a scenario named after a domain, adding its parents and siblings for valid keys. Section 6.25
RangeGen / RandomGen (GenRocket)
Number generators: RangeGen counts up (or down); RandomGen picks a random number in a range. Section 6.18
Receiver (GenRocket)
The component that turns raw generated values into a usable output: file, SQL, message and more. Section 6.23
Receiver parameter (GenRocket)
A setting for the whole receiver, such as where to write; a star marks required ones. Section 6.22
Reference parameter (GenRocket)
A parameter value pointing at another attribute or generator, so one value is built from others. Section 7.14
ReferenceGen / ProxyReferenceGen (GenRocket)
The foreign-key pair: the proxy marks "a parent key goes here" on the domain; the real ReferenceGen replaces it in a scenario. Section 6.18
Scenario chain (GenRocket)
Linked scenarios run in order by one command; a chain set links chains. Section 6.25
Scenario domain (GenRocket)
A scenario's copy of a template domain; template changes flow down unless locked. Section 6.13
Scenario (GenRocket)
A saved instruction sheet: which domains to generate, how many rows, in what order. Running it makes the data. Section 6.25
Scratch Pad (GenRocket)
A way to create a domain from a domain name and a list of attribute names, optionally with type and size; generators are assigned automatically. Section 6.14.3
Seed (GenRocket)
A starting number for random generators; the same seed gives the same "random" data. Every domain has a seed variable. Section 6.18
Segment-and-merge receivers (GenRocket)
Pairs that build nested JSON or XML: each domain writes segments, a merge receiver combines them. Section 6.24
SimplePatternGen (GenRocket)
The fallback generator making values like name1, name2. Treat it as a to-do marker. Section 6.18
SQLFileInsertReceiver (GenRocket)
Writes a file of SQL insert statements without touching a database; a safe first choice. Section 6.24
Stateful scenario (GenRocket)
A scenario that remembers generator positions between runs, so a counter continues. Section 6.25
Synthetic Data Replacement (GenRocket)
GenRocket's masking method: replacing sensitive values with synthetic ones. Section 6.24
Template domain (GenRocket)
A domain as defined in a project version, the master copy scenarios copy from. Section 6.14
Test Data Automation (GenRocket)
GenRocket's phrase for defining test data once as components and generating it automatically, whenever a test needs it. Section 6.4
Test data case suite (GenRocket)
In the 2022 demo, a suite of unit, negative, load and integration cases built by a self-service module, with volume set per domain and rules controlling the output. Section 6.4.2
Test Data Engineer (GenRocket)
A trained person who models test data projects and designs the data for others. Section 3.9
Test data project (GenRocket)
The data model for one application or database (domains, attributes, generators, receivers, scenarios) with no customer data; also the unit of licensing. Section 6.8
Test data rules, G-Rules (GenRocket)
Condition-and-action rules attached to cases rather than single attributes, suited to complex if/then/else logic. Section 6.18.5
Test plan (GenRocket)
A set of scenarios run with one command, as when a Jenkins job runs GenRocket against a test plan. Section 6.25.10
User profile and server profile (GenRocket)
A .grp file in the .genrocket folder that ties a person, or a CI/CD server, to a licence. Section 6.6
Visible (GenRocket)
An attribute setting; if false, receivers do not see it, so it works as a helper. Section 7.14
Web platform (GenRocket)
GenRocket's cloud web application, where you model and design. Also called GenRocket Cloud. Section 6.1
WeightGen / MultiWeightGen (GenRocket)
Percentage-based choice: WeightGen splits between two values, MultiWeightGen among any number. Section 7.5
XTS (GenRocket)
Extract Table Schema: a runtime option that writes a live database's structure, not data, to an encrypted file. Section 6.14

10.30 Delphix

Algorithm (Delphix)
The recipe that replaces a real value with a safe one. Profiling only chooses it; the masking job runs it. Section 8.39
Algorithm framework (Delphix)
A kind of algorithm you configure into instances, such as Secure Lookup or Segment Mapping. Section 9.1
Algorithm instance (Delphix)
A named framework plus settings, such as dlpx-core:FirstName. This is what you assign to a column. Section 9.1
Algorithm key (Delphix)
A secret value inside most algorithms; changing it changes every output. Section 9.1
Algorithm syncability (Delphix)
Copying algorithms between engines so both mask alike. Non-deterministic ones cannot be synced. Section 8.35
Application (Delphix)
A tag naming the system whose data you manage; create one before an environment. Section 8.16
ASDD profiler (Delphix)
Automated Sensitive Data Discovery, the current profiler, using classifiers instead of the legacy search expressions. Section 8.39
ASDD Standard (Delphix)
The recommended built-in profile set, with path, type, regex and list classifiers. Section 8.43
Assignment threshold (Delphix)
The minimum confidence before the profiler writes a domain and algorithm into the inventory. Section 8.39
Async Task (Delphix)
A background task whose ID you can search under Monitor, Async Tasks. Section 8.4
Character Mapping (Delphix)
A keyed one-to-one swap of characters within groups you define; unlisted characters stay. Suits key columns. Section 9.4
Checklist (Delphix)
An action checking that every table in a rule set has a primary key, an indexed identity column or a logical key. Section 8.34
Classifier (Delphix)
One recognition rule tied to one domain, such as a column name that looks like ssn. Section 8.39
Classifier framework (Delphix)
A kind of classifier: PATH (table and column name), TYPE (data type and length), REGEX (data) or LIST (values in a list). Section 2.7
Collision (Delphix)
Two different real values masked to the same fake value. Section 8.7
Confidence (Delphix)
A 0 to 100 score for how sure the profiler is about a match. Section 8.39
Connector (Delphix)
Saved details the engine uses to reach one database or set of files. Section 8.17
Containerized engine (Delphix)
The engine run as a Kubernetes pod of three containers; meant for ephemeral use, and without FTP file masking. Section 8.8
Continuous Compliance engine (Delphix)
The product that discovers sensitive data and masks it; also called the masking engine or Compliance Engine. Section 8.1
Continuous Data engine (Delphix)
The virtualization product that creates VDBs; never run it in the same machine as the masking engine. Section 8.23
Copybook (Delphix)
A rule set type for mainframe data sets described by a copybook. Section 8.30
Date Shift (Delphix)
Moves a date within a range, never back onto itself. Fixed shifts are deterministic; variable ones are not syncable. Section 9.9
Dependent Date Shift (Delphix)
Masks two related dates together, keeping their order and interval. Section 9.4
Domain (Delphix)
A named kind of sensitive data, such as FIRST_NAME, with exactly one default masking algorithm. Section 8.1
dSource (Delphix)
The engine's compressed copy of a source database from which VDBs are made. Section 8.23
Encoding (Delphix)
How characters become bytes. ASCII and UTF-8 for most file types; mainframe data must be EBCDIC. Section 9.17
End-of-record (Delphix)
The characters ending a line: LF on Linux, CR+LF on Windows. Section 9.15
Environment (Delphix)
A folder-like container inside an application holding connectors, rule sets and jobs. Its purpose is Mask or Tokenize/Re-Identify. Section 8.16
Extended connector (Delphix)
A connector on an uploaded JDBC driver, for databases not supported natively. Section 8.15
File connector and file rule set (Delphix)
The connector says where files are; the rule set lists which files or name patterns and which format each uses. Section 9.15
File format (Delphix)
A small uploaded description of a file's field names (and lengths for fixed width). Metadata only. Section 9.19
Free Text Redaction (Delphix)
Blanks sensitive words and patterns inside notes columns; needs expertise to configure. Section 2.32
Identity column (Delphix)
A column that numbers rows automatically; the engine can use one as the key or add a temporary one. Section 8.27
In-place / on-the-fly (Delphix)
In-place changes data where it sits; on-the-fly reads a source and writes masked data to a target. Section 9.11
Inventory (Delphix)
The table of every column or field in a rule set with its domain and algorithm: profiler output, masking input. Section 8.39
JDBC (Delphix)
Java Database Connectivity. The engine uses a JDBC driver per database and a JDBC URL to say where to connect. Section 8.15
Job (Delphix)
A saved runnable task: profiling, masking, tokenization or re-identification. Section 8.1
Logical key (Delphix)
A unique, non-null column (or columns) you tell Delphix to treat as the row identifier; the database is unchanged. Section 8.33
Lookup file (Delphix)
A text file with one replacement value per line; the algorithm picks a line from the input. Section 9.1
Mapping (Delphix)
You supply replacement values; each input gets a distinct output, stored encrypted. Needs enough values. Section 9.9
Masked VDB (Delphix)
A VDB whose data was masked by a masking job during provisioning; it cannot be unmasked. Section 8.23
Masking engine (Delphix)
The Continuous Compliance engine, where connectors, rule sets, profiling jobs and masking jobs are defined. Section 9.30
Masking job (Delphix)
A saved set of options to apply a rule set's inventory, with a method, threads, memory and scripts. Section 9.1
Masking user (Delphix)
A dedicated database account for the connector, with only the permissions masking needs. Section 8.15
Match strength (Delphix)
A weight on one classifier match, such as 0.7 for a dashed SSN; several weak clues combine. Section 2.7
Meta Data Source (Delphix)
The connector screen grouping: Database, File or Mainframe. Section 8.15
Min Max (Delphix)
Pulls extreme values toward the middle, since the top salary may reveal a person. Section 9.4
Multi-Tenant (Delphix)
A job option letting one rule set serve identical schemas through different connectors chosen at run time. Section 8.23
Non-conformant data (Delphix)
A value not fitting the expected format. By default it stays unmasked and only a warning is recorded, so check job warnings. Section 8.7
Out-of-the-box instance (Delphix)
An instance shipping with the engine; it cannot be edited or deleted. Section 9.1
Payment Card (Delphix)
Keeps the first digits and the card's Luhn validity, masking the rest. Section 9.4
Profile set (Delphix)
A bundle of classifiers; choosing one decides which kinds of sensitive data a job can detect. Section 8.39
Profiling (Delphix)
Scanning column names and optionally data to find sensitive columns. Also called sensitive data discovery. Section 8.39
Profiling job (Delphix)
A saved instruction to scan a rule set using a profile set. Section 8.39
Record type and qualifier (Delphix)
When one file mixes layouts, a record type names each layout and up to three qualifiers recognise it. Section 9.20
Role (Delphix)
A set of allowed actions on object kinds; each user has one role and a list of environments. Section 8.9
ROWID (Delphix)
An Oracle pseudocolumn holding each row's address; recommended as the logical key for speed. Section 8.27
Rule set (Delphix)
The list of tables or files of one connector that jobs may look at. Four types: Database, Semi-Structured, Flat Files, Copybook. Section 8.30
SDD (Selective Data Distribution) (Delphix)
Replicating a masked VDB to another engine in non-production without sending unmasked data. Section 8.15
Secure Lookup (Delphix)
Picks a replacement from a lookup file by hashing the input, so output is consistent. Collisions are possible. Section 8.7
SecureShuffle (Delphix)
Moves each value to a different row; the values stay real and the result is not syncable. Section 2.32
Segment Mapping (Delphix)
Masks short values by segments with no collisions, if the allowed set is big enough; suits SSNs and keys. Section 8.7
Snapshot and TimeFlow (Delphix)
A snapshot is a dataset's state at one moment; the TimeFlow is the timeline of those states. Section 8.23
Stream (Delphix)
One parallel lane of work; more streams let the engine process several tables at once. Section 9.1
String Algorithm Chain (Delphix)
Runs two to eight string algorithms one after another. Section 2.32
Text enclosure and escape (Delphix)
The enclosure (often a double quote) wraps a value that may contain the delimiter; the escape writes a literal enclosure. Section 9.15
Tokenization (Delphix)
The only reversible family, using AES-128 and Base64; output is longer than input. Reversing it is re-identification. Section 9.9
Update threads (Delphix)
Parallel threads writing to the target database; too many can deadlock and each table needs an index. Section 9.11
VDB (virtual database) (Delphix)
A full read-write copy of source data provisioned from a dSource or another VDB, created in minutes. Section 8.23
Virtual appliance (Delphix)
A ready-made machine image of the engine imported into a hypervisor or cloud. Section 8.8
Whole-file masking (Delphix)
Sending a file to one algorithm in one piece, using a format with one field of length 0. Section 9.26
