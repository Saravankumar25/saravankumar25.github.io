29.8 The proof of concept

A POC answers one question: will this approach work here, on our data, with our people? It is small on purpose. It uses a copy, never production, and it ends with a pass or fail against criteria written before it started. A POC without agreed criteria turns into a demo that everyone declares a success. General concept

POC plan for ShopSphere

Delphix leaves non-conformant values unmasked by default and records a warning, so a job can finish while data stays exposed (see Delphix masking terminology). Informatica's SSN masking is documented to be unable to return all-unique values (User Guide 10.5.10, p.175), which is exactly the sort of limit a POC should expose before rollout.

How a services partner structures the whole path

From video The POC is the middle of a longer path. In the 2020 GenRocket and Kualitatem webinar, a representative of Kualitatem, a software-testing services company and GenRocket partner, described how his company structures a TDM engagement in three phases. This is one company's method, told in a sales webinar, not a standard. It does match the templates in this chapter.

Partner's phaseWhat the partner describedMatching templates here
1. AssessmentBuild the business case. Scan the client's environments, tools and skills. Recommend a TDM tool and give an implementation plan for it.The use-case questionnaire (29.4), the landscape inventory (29.5), the tool matrix (29.7) and the assessment report (29.9)
2. Pilot, then roadmap and rolloutPick a medium-sized project, neither the biggest nor the smallest, that covers the key scenarios, and produce results the client can see as measurable improvement. Then lay out a roadmap and roll out project by project, based on the data model.The POC plan and its success criteria (29.8), then the deployment plan (29.11)
3. SupportAfter go-live, a set period of production support in which the client can bring problems and questions back to the team.The Operate phase of the deployment plan, and the conditions and owners recorded at sign-off (29.12)

Two points are worth borrowing. First, choose the pilot for coverage, not for ease: a project that touches the key scenarios shows the client the real benefit, while a trivial one proves little. This chapter's ShopSphere POC is deliberately smaller (a lab copy, ten days), whereas the partner's pilot is a real medium-sized project; both exist to produce measurable results before a wider rollout. Second, plan the support period before go-live, so the client knows who to call after the project team leaves.

The partner also listed what it weighs when recommending a tool: complexity and cost, speed of providing data, adaptability to the client's existing tools (it named Jenkins, Selenium, CI pipelines and programming languages), and the support available. These map onto the scoring table in section 29.7, which gains a support row for this reason. The partner added that GenRocket meets all four factors. That is a claim from a vendor's partner, so take scores from your own POC instead.

The webinar ended with the vendor's route to a POC: a personal demo, then a no-cost proof of concept delivered by the vendor, the partner or both. Vendors commonly offer a similar route. Accept it if it helps, but still agree your own success criteria and exit decision first.

29.9 The assessment of a line of business

Day 54 ends with an assessment on "a particular LOB". The POC proves the tool; the assessment describes the business area it will be applied to, and what must change for a full rollout. For ShopSphere the LOB is online order management. Work through the dimensions below and record evidence for each. General concept

DimensionWhat to find out
Application and interfacesWhich applications use the data, which files and APIs leave the LOB, which hold copies
DataTables, volumes, growth, real keys and undeclared relationships (a join the application uses but the database does not declare breaks masking)
Environments and refreshHow many, how often, who triggers it, current duration
Existing controlsCurrent masking scripts, access rules, past incidents
ComplianceApplicable rules and what evidence an auditor will ask for
Fit with toolsLandscape rows against tool support, from the matrix
InfrastructureCapacity and network. Delphix's best-practice page, for example, gives a standard dedicated engine as 8 vCPUs and at least 64 GB of RAM, about one vCPU per concurrent masking job and at least 1 GB per job (Best practices)
People and processWho will own rules, keys and reruns after the project

Assessment report outline

  1. Summary: scope, verdict (ready, ready with conditions, not ready), the three findings that matter most.
  2. Scope and method: LOB, systems, interviews held, evidence reviewed, POC results.
  3. Current state: the landscape inventory and the use-case register.
  4. Sensitivity results: the column ratings and decisions.
  5. Findings: each with evidence, severity and a recommendation, for example "F3, High: the partner file L3 carries unmasked SSNs; mask before the next nightly run".
  6. Tool recommendation: the filled scoring table and why.
  7. Deployment plan: phases, effort and dependencies (next section).
  8. Risks: the register.
  9. Appendices: evidence pack, queries, configuration exports.
