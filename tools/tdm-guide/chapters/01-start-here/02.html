The guide has five parts. The order is deliberate: each part uses ideas from the one before.

Part I is the longest in concepts and the shortest in clicking. Do not skip it. Every tool chapter assumes you know what a primary key is, what masking is, and why masked values must stay consistent. Those ideas are taught once, in Part I, and then reused.

Three reading routes

Pick the one that matches you.

Route A: complete beginner

Read Chapters 1 to 9 in order. Then follow the 60-day plan below. Do every lab.

Route B: you know databases and SQL

Skim Chapter 3 (do its quiz), read Chapters 2 and 4 to 7 properly, skim 8 and 9, then go to the tool parts.

Route C: your project uses one tool

Read Chapters 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 for the concepts, then jump to that tool's part. Come back for the others later.

Route D: revision before an evaluation

Use each chapter's "Recap" and "Check yourself", then Chapter 31 and the glossary.

1.6 How to read a chapter

Every chapter has the same skeleton

Once you know the skeleton you can find anything quickly.

  1. Opening paragraph and "What you will learn".

    Tells you where the chapter fits and why you should care.

  2. Words to know first.

    A short glossary of the terms the chapter uses. Read it before the rest; it makes the body easier.

  3. Core sections.

    Each major topic goes: the concept, a simple explanation, why it matters, an example, a picture, then steps and the expected result.

  4. Common mistakes and gotchas.

    Orange boxes listing what trips people up.

  5. Recap.

    A handful of bullets. Good for revision.

  6. Check yourself.

    Three to five questions. Click a question to reveal the answer and the reason.

  7. Sources used in this chapter.

    The documents I actually read for that chapter, and what each was used for.

What the coloured boxes mean

Note

Background that helps but is not essential. Blue.

Tip

A shortcut or good habit.

Why this matters

The reason behind a step or concept. If you remember only one thing from a section, remember this box.

Example

A worked example, usually using ShopSphere (see 1.9).

Warning

A mistake people commonly make.

Recap

A summary of what you should now know.

Evidence badges: how sure is each statement?

This guide separates three kinds of statement, because mixing them is how misinformation spreads.

From docs
Taken from an official source I read: the Informatica User Guide PDF, or a Delphix or GenRocket help page. The chapter names the page.
General concept
Widely used industry knowledge that is not tied to one product. Not from a vendor page.
Unverified
Something I could not confirm in the sources. Treat it as a question to put to your trainer or test in your own tool.

Tool chapters also begin with a note titled "What this chapter is based on", naming the exact sources and versions.

Pictures: what is official and what is drawn here

  • Original diagrams are drawn for this guide. Their captions start with "Original diagram".
  • Schematic sketches of a tool screen carry a note that they are illustrations, not screenshots.
  • Official screenshots are used for Delphix and GenRocket only. They are not copied into this site; the page loads the image from the vendor's own help site and links back to the source page. If a vendor moves an image, it may disappear from here.
  • Informatica figures are not embedded. You will see a grey dashed "Visual reference" box that names the PDF page and figure and says what to look for. If you have the User Guide open, you can look at the page yourself.

Search, theme and navigation

  • Search. Press / anywhere on a page (or click the Search button in the top bar). Type a word such as "primary key" or "shuffle". Use the arrow keys to move through results, Enter to open one, Esc to close. It searches the whole guide, section by section.
  • Theme toggle. The round button at the top right switches between light and dark. Your choice is remembered in your browser.
  • Sidebar. On a wide screen the left sidebar lists all chapters and, below them, the sections of the page you are on. On a phone it collapses above the text.
  • Progress bar. The thin bar at the very top shows how far down the chapter you are.
  • Previous / next links at the bottom of each chapter follow the learning path.

The syllabus also lists mandatory learning modules (Data Masking, Data Sub-setting and Data Generation "101-Basics"), eight hands-on exercises and three project activities. They are all mapped on the guide home page and covered in the same chapters.

How to pace a day

A chapter is a 15 to 20 minute read, but a lab takes longer. A workable pattern for a tool day: read the concept chapter (20 minutes), read the tool chapter (30 minutes), do the lab (the rest of the day). Do not try to read two tool chapters and do no labs; the labs are where the tool knowledge sticks.

Expect the syllabus to overlap

Several rows use the same chapters (for example Chapter 29 appears on Days 43, 48, 54 and 57). That is intentional: planning is learnt in layers, not in one go.
